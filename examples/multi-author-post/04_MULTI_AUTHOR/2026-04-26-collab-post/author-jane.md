# A Pattern That Works

If the split is real, file-per-author drafting beats "we'll just edit a shared doc" every time. The reason is friction: shared editing makes both authors timid, each one trying not to step on the other's voice. Separate files let each writer commit to their angle without negotiating in real time.

## The merge step

When the per-author files are ready, a deterministic merge produces the published version. Attribution survives in the merged file as comment markers — invisible to readers, useful to editors who need to know who wrote what.

The merge isn't smart; it's honest. It doesn't smooth voice transitions; it flags them. Editorial smoothing is a separate, deliberate pass — one author or both deciding together where the seams need work.
